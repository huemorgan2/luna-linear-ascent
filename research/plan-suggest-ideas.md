# THE SUGGESTION — read this, decide, ignore the catalogue below until needed

## 0 · The premise fix, not optional: One Tower (26)

Roy's ruling: **the one-universe issue is a flaw to fix — this game is
multiplayer, only.** So One Tower is not the fourth pick on a list; it is the
premise everything else sits inside. **There are only 100 wardens, all
shared** — the first climber to fell warden 1 clears the path for everyone,
same as warden 71. Floors 1–30 are tuned so one amazing player *can* solo
the shared warden (regen below one player's output — hard, achievable); from
31 the wound out-heals any single blade, so solo is impossible by
arithmetic. The era ends together at 100; reincarnation persists. Full
design: `one-tower-for-everyone.md`.

## The picks — five things, five clocks

| Pick | Idea | Clock | The player's sentence |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1st** | **Contract board** (1) | daily | "what are today's three jobs?" |
| **2nd** | **Nightly rejuvenation** (14, Roy's cut of it) | dawn | "I'm healed — a new day" |
| **3rd** | **Night slot** (8, simplified: rest *or* work — professions later) | nightly | "what do I do with tonight?" |
| **4th** | **Weekly strongbox** (2) | weekly | "which reward do I take this week?" |
| **5th** | **The war** — One Tower's floor-by-floor sieges | the era | "how goes the war upstairs?" |

Each lives on a **different clock** — daily, dawn, nightly, weekly, era — which
was the core WoW lesson: many independent rhythms, never one shared meter.
Together with hunting itself that is six rhythms, which *feels* like a full
game while no single system is deep enough to confuse. No new currencies, no
skill trees, no new stats.

**On nightly rejuvenation (Roy's addition, and he is right about the sink):**
HP restores at the night boundary — you sleep, you heal — rather than trickling
on a 36–48h clock. More real, and it protects the potion sink *better* than the
slow-trickle version: **during the day there is no free healing at all**, so
mid-session potions still cost gold and still buy time; the free reset only
arrives with dawn. It generalises the Lodge's existing +20-at-dawn into the
law of the world, and it nests perfectly inside the night slot — everyone heals
overnight, and choosing *rest* banks rested aether on top.

**Underneath, not player-facing:** 13 (floor/level split, planned as 021), then
the Grand Retune (10 + 11 + 26's coordination curve) — one tuning pass, once.
**Free housekeeping any time:** 5, 12, 18.

## The onboarding ladder — one new system at a time

The existing game (hunt, warden, town) **is** the tutorial; nothing new before
level 4. One reveal per band, announced through plan 020's "what opens next"
line so each arrival is expected:

| When | What appears | Why there |
|---|---|---|
| Level 1–3 | nothing new — hunt, gear, your first wardens | protect the first hour |
| Day 2 | **nightly rejuvenation** — noticed, not taught ("dawn — your wounds have closed") | passive, zero cognitive load |
| Level ~4 | **the contract board** | first daily habit, right when the grind sets in |
| Level ~6 | **the night slot** at the Lodge | by now the player has felt the energy wall overnight |
| Level ~10 | **the strongbox** at the Vault | one gear band in — ready for a weekly rhythm |
| Floor 31 | **the war** — first shared warden | the mid-game reveal: the game is one world |
| Era end | **reincarnation** | needs no teaching until the day it happens |

## If we add ONE more thing (Roy asked: complexity aside, best for players)

**Hunting parties — fighting side by side, together in the same battle (27).**

Reasoning: One Tower makes the *world* shared, but the core verb — fighting —
stays solo, and striking a shared HP pool is parallel play, not together play.
Every lasting MMO memory is "the night WE killed it", made of named people in
the same fight. A party of two or three in one battle — your blows and your
friend's in the same round log, the warden turning on whoever drew it — is the
single highest-value thing this game could add for players: it converts every
system already on this page (contracts, sieges, factions, the war) from
*adjacent* play into *shared* play. It is also the hardest item here by far
(synchronous multi-player combat in an async chat game), which is why it is the
answer to "complexity aside" and not in the five picks. Runner-up: professions
(7) — identity and interdependence ("I'm the world's blacksmith"), at a tenth
of the cost.

Everything else — professions ranks (7), salvage (3), per-fight bonuses (4),
hirelings (9), the regen stat (17), the escalating cell (19), structures (24) —
is **deferred, on purpose**: each is only worth adding when players say "I've
seen it all."

# Details

## A · More ways to earn

### 1 · Contract board

**What.** Three contracts posted in Roothollow each day. "Bring down four
ash-salamanders on floor 3." "Six kills with arrows." "Strike the Warden once."
Gold and XP on completion, and ideally a gear-tier token.

**How it works.** A daily seeded triple (same seed pattern as the pawn rate, so
the board is identical for everyone that day and can be discussed). Each
contract is a counter plus a target, checked against the existing combat ledger
rather than new bookkeeping. Claim at the board; the day's set expires at the
world-day tick.

**Why first.** It is the only idea here that **multiplies the fights you already
have** instead of asking for more. Energy caps you near 30 fights a day; a
contract makes some of those 30 pay double. It is also the biggest hole in the
game — there is no quest system of any kind — and the hooks exist: `BOARD_PRICE
= 10` is defined and never used, floor YAML already contains bounty prose wired
to nothing, and `vision/economy.md` specced a notice board that was dropped back
in plan 004.

**Depends on.** Nothing. Ideally lands after 13.

**Risk.** Low. The daily count is a natural cap, so it cannot become a grind.

### 2 · Weekly strongbox at the Vault

**What.** WoW's Great Vault, at a building you already have. During the week you
cross thresholds in three activities the game already counts — kills, floors
climbed, warden strikes — at 2/4/6. Each threshold opens a slot. At the weekly
tick you **pick exactly one** reward from the slots you opened: a gold lump, an
aether lump, a gear-tier token, a relic.

**How it works.** Three counters on the player record, reset weekly. The Vault
card shows which slots are lit and what each would pay. Choosing takes one.

**Why.** It costs **no energy**, so it does not compete with hunting. It rewards
breadth rather than repetition. And it converts a random week into a guaranteed
payoff with agency — the reason the Great Vault is WoW's retention spine is that
a bad-luck week still moves you forward because *you* choose.

**Depends on.** Nothing. Keep it distinct from the faction weekly challenge,
which is collective — this one is personal.

**Risk.** Low, if the reward table is priced against a week of income.

### 3 · Salvage

**What.** Break gear you would otherwise pawn into materials that discount
honing.

**How it works.** A second option next to the pawn shop's sell. Materials are a
new inventory count spent at the Forge to reduce `hone_price`.

**Why.** It is the local version of WoW's gathering loop: a non-combat route
where accumulated junk becomes progress. Today pawn value is the only exit for
unwanted gear, and pawn pays 25–55%.

**Depends on.** Nothing.

**Risk.** Low. Watch that it does not undercut the pawn shop entirely — one
should be gold-now, the other progress-later.

### 4 · Per-fight bonuses

**What.** Small rewards for quality rather than quantity: a kill streak, the
first kill of the day, a clean kill with no damage taken.

**How it works.** Flags on the daily record, read at `_victory`.

**Why.** Energy is the binding constraint, so *anything* that pays more per
fight is high leverage — and unlike a bigger energy bar, this rewards skill and
kit rather than patience.

**Depends on.** Nothing. Cheapest real win on this list after 5.

**Risk.** Low, but it stacks with 1 — price them together so a contract kill
that is also a clean streak kill does not pay four times.

### 5 · The two dead flags

**What.** `repair_token` is granted by presents (`core.py:251-252`) and has **no
spend path anywhere**. `rumor_day` is set by the present roll and **never read**.

**How it works.** Either give `repair_token` a use at the Forge (a free mend) or
remove it from the present table; either make `rumor_day` do something in combat
or delete it.

**Why.** These are rewards the player is already being handed that do nothing.
Plan 019 already flags the first.

**Risk.** None. Do this whenever.

## B · The offline night

### 6 · Rested aether

**What.** Time away banks a pool that boosts XP per kill until spent.

**How it works.** Accrue from time since last seen, cap the pool hard (a few
days' worth), spend automatically on kills. **Kills only** — never contract or
strongbox payouts, or a rested week double-dips through every route at once.

**Why.** The energy cap is `24 + level//10` against 45-minute regen, so past
about 18 hours away your regen **overflows and is silently lost**. The game
currently punishes long absences. This inverts the same absence into banked
value, which is a stated goal: "a break never punishes below baseline."

**Depends on.** Best delivered as part of 8.

**Risk.** The multiplier, not the mechanic. WoW's +200% works because levelling
there is a phase before the real game; here the climb *is* the game (a season,
~165 days), and XP is deliberately the scarcest resource. Use a smaller number
and a hard cap.

### 7 · Professions

**What.** Study a trade — blacksmith at the Forge, bartender at the Lodge,
guildmaster at the Guildhall — then earn from it while offline. Ranks unlock
more.

**How it works.** Study progresses a rank; a rank raises what a night's work
pays. **Pay mostly in materials and access** — forge discounts, potion inputs,
contract unlocks — rather than raw coin (see risk).

**Why.** It is the *Jones in the Fast Lane* loop, and it is the only route here
that ignores the energy cap entirely. The job sites all already exist in the town
square, so no new locations are needed.

**The catch, and the fix.** In *Jones*, studying **competes** with earning — an
hour at university is an hour not working, and that tension is the game. Here
offline time is free, so "study overnight" costs nothing you feel, and a cost you
don't feel isn't a cost: the tree would just unlock itself on a calendar with no
decision made. **That is what 8 fixes.**

**Depends on.** 8, absolutely. And 1, if professions are ever to give quests —
there is no quest system for them to ride on.

**Risk.** Offline gold reaches XP through the Energy cell, so it *is* a
progression faucet. It is acceptable for the same reason the cell is: a hard
daily ceiling. **Never let profession income raise the one-cell-a-day limit.**
Second risk is gear inflation — hence paying in materials rather than coin.

**Scope warning.** Three professions × a rank tree each × per-profession quests
is plausibly bigger than combat. Ship **one profession, three ranks, no
profession quests**. Add breadth only once people choose "work" over "rest".

### 8 · The night slot ← the one I'd build

**What.** One action per night, three choices: **rest** (bank aether for XP),
**study** (progress a profession rank), or **work** (gold now).

**How it works.** On return, the game resolves the night you chose. Choosing the
next night is a single option at the Lodge.

**Why this is the good idea.** It does five things at once:

1. Ideas 6 and 7 stop being two systems and become **one**.
2. Studying regains a real price — a night studying is a night not resting and
   not earning. *Jones*'s tension, restored exactly.
3. It is **self-capping**: one night, one action. No rate to tune, no grind.
4. It gives the Lodge a reason to exist beyond defence (today it is ◈10 × level
   for offline PvP protection and +20 HP at dawn).
5. It puts a **real decision in every day** — which is the actual complaint. Play
   today is identical to play yesterday; this makes your *absence* a choice.

**Depends on.** Nothing structurally, but it is much better after 10/11, because
what a night is worth depends on whether XP is still a gate.

**Risk.** Low. This is the shape that makes 6 and 7 safe.

### 9 · Hireling jobs

**What.** Send your sidekick or a hired hand on a timed errand for gold.

**How it works.** Start a job, collect on return. No tree, no ranks.

**Why.** It is 7's payoff without 7's scope. Already named as deferred in
`plans/003-execution/phase-1/summary.md`.

**Depends on.** Nothing. A reasonable substitute if 7 looks too big.

**Risk.** Same faucet caution as 7.

## C · The two ladders

### 10 · Cap player level

**What.** A level cap around **30**, reached in the first weeks rather than
never.

**How it works.** Add `LEVEL_CAP` — **there is none today**, level is unbounded —
and compress the XP curve so the cap arrives early.

**Why 30.** Three things already agree on that number: `WARDEN_SOFT_FLOOR = 30`
is commented "last floor tuned for solo play"; the solo win curve is 65–85%
through floor 30 and under 10% before floor 50; and gear bands are ten floors
wide. Capping there means **the level game ends exactly where the cooperative
game begins.**

**The prize.** Level becomes WoW's Ladder 1: fast, bounded, satisfying, the thing
that produces commitment. And **XP stops being a gate and becomes a currency** —
it is already spendable on spells, scans and honing, so at cap every kill's XP
flows into those instead of a bar that stopped moving. That also dissolves the
whole gold→XP anxiety: once XP is a consumable, letting gold reach it is far less
dangerous.

**Depends on.** **13, mandatory.** See the blocker there.

**Risk.** This is a re-tuning of the entire economy, not an afternoon. It needs
its own plan and a spreadsheet pass, and it should land **before** 1 and 8,
because those pay in gold and XP whose meaning it redefines.

### 11 · Gear becomes the power ladder

**What.** Move the majority of power growth from level to gear, so floors 31–100
still have a ladder.

**How it works.** Three changes, in order of size:

- **Weapon bonus must scale far harder** than today's `8 × T` (tier 10 is only
  +80). Something nearer `30 × T`.
- **Armor must contribute HP.** Today max HP is `40 + 12 × level` — **100%
  level** — so a capped player flatlines at 400 HP for seventy floors. This is
  the biggest single piece of surgery on this list.
- **Energy cap must stop riding `level // 10`**, or it freezes at +3.

**Why.** Because today level supplies about **72% of attack at floor 20 and 77%
at floor 100**, and all of HP. Gear is currently the garnish. Capping level
without this removes three quarters of all power growth.

**The reassurance.** Monsters and wardens are *derived from* the player model —
`_at_level_loadout` is "the reference all tuning points at" — so fixing the
reference propagates to every enemy automatically. Much less frightening than it
sounds.

**Depends on.** 10 and 13.

**Risk.** High, and unavoidable if 10 happens. They are one plan.

### 12 · Floor number as public status

**What.** Make the floor you have reached visible and socially meaningful — on
the Stone, in the roster, wherever climbers see each other.

**How it works.** Mostly surfacing what exists. `_stone_scene` already prints
"highest floor opened".

**Why.** Item level works in WoW partly because it is **public** and gates who
will group with you. A private number cannot carry status. This is the cheap half
of making 11 feel like a ladder rather than bookkeeping.

**Depends on.** Nothing, but it is what gives 11 its point.

**Risk.** None.

### 13 · Split floor from level in code — **planned as 021**

**What.** Make it impossible to pass a floor where a level is expected. Zero
behaviour change.

**The blocker it removes.** `gear_level_req(tier)` returns `band_start(tier)` — a
**floor** — and uses it as a **level requirement**. Cap level at 30 and gear
tiers 4–10 become permanently unbuyable: the gear ladder dies exactly when it is
meant to take over. And `warden_stats` calls `player_max_hp(floor)`, passing a
floor into a level parameter, so with a cap of 30 a floor-80 warden would be
tuned against 1,000 HP when the real player has 400 — **off by 2.5×, silently,
with no type checker in the plugin to catch it.**

**Status.** `plugin-linear-ascent/plans/021-floor-level-disambiguation/plan.md`.

**Do this first if you do 10 or 11 at all.**

## D · Healing and time

### 14 · Out-of-combat rejuvenation

**What.** HP returns on its own over 24h or more. Healing purchases become a way
to skip that wait.

**How it works.** Lazy accrual from `last_seen`, exactly like the warden's regen
already does. No new clock.

**Why.** Your framing: *"healing costs money — buys time, or not if you don't
need it."* The Lodge's +20 HP at dawn is already a crude version of this, so the
precedent exists.

**Depends on.** 15, or it breaks the economy.

**Risk.** It erodes a designed gold sink — see 15.

### 15 · HP regenerates slower than energy

**What.** A full heal takes ~36–48 hours while energy fills in ~18.

**Why this matters more than it looks.** Energy fills in about 18 hours. If HP
also restores in ~24, anyone returning from a real break arrives with **full HP
and full energy** and will never buy a potion again — the healing sink dies, and
`vision/economy.md` §9's drain budget goes with it. Making HP the slower meter
puts the mid-session player reliably in the state *"I have energy I dare not
spend"*, which is exactly when a potion is worth real money.

**This is the knob that turns 14 from a nice sentence into an economy.**

### 16 · No mid-fight regeneration — **decided**

**What.** We are not adding in-combat healing over time.

**Why, for the record.** It deletes three problems at once:

- `WARDEN_DMG_BUDGET = 1.07` means a warden deals 107% of your HP pool across
  ~12 rounds, i.e. **~8.9% per round** — so regenerating ~9% of max HP per round
  makes you literally unkillable, and the fight never ends. The safe window is a
  single-digit percentage: the stat does nothing until it does everything.
- Combat is **player-paced** and fight state persists between messages, so
  *time-based* regen means waiting between rounds heals you to full. Every fight
  becomes unloseable for anyone patient.
- Even 2% per round is a ~22% effective-HP swing, which forces
  `WARDEN_DMG_BUDGET` — the constant the whole 65–85% solo win curve rests on —
  to be re-derived.

### 17 · Rejuvenation as an endgame stat, on a per-fight budget

**What.** Your "only for the super advanced" version, done safely: recover up to
N% of your pool **per fight**, spent automatically as you take damage, with a
longer fight yielding **no more** healing.

**How it works.** A charge budget, not a rate. Duration stops being the lever,
which kills both the immortality cliff and the stall exploit from 16.

**Why it belongs.** A stat that is **gear-, relic- and faction-driven and never
level-driven** is a textbook Ladder 2 stat. It gives the post-cap gear ladder a
second axis besides raw ATK and DEF, which is what stops item-level-style
progression from being one boring number.

**The house pattern to copy.** Speed already grants dodge, and the answer was
`DODGE_CAP_PCT = 12` — "speed never becomes the main defense" — as log-decay from
advantage only, fully visible on the `[i]` card. Rejuvenation should inherit all
three: a 1–10 authorable scale, log-decay with a hard cap, no hidden numbers.

**Depends on.** 10/11. Introduce it there so the balance constant is re-derived
once rather than twice.

## E · Gold and tempo

### 18 · "Gold buys time, never power"

**What.** Adopt one sentence as economic law.

**Why.** It is already true of the best thing in the economy: the Energy cell
does not give you power, it gives you **five energy you would otherwise have
waited for**. Your healing framing is the same shape. The cell buys energy-time,
a potion buys HP-time, the Lodge buys safety-time. Honing and gear are the
exception that proves the rule — they buy power, and they are gated on *floor*,
which is earned.

**What it settles.** The "no gold → XP faucet" rule has been quietly false since
the cell shipped, and we spent an hour circling it. This replaces it with
something both true and defensible: **gold cannot buy progress, only earlier
progress.** One sentence a player can hold in their head.

**Risk.** None. It is a decision, not code — but write it into
`vision/economy.md` or it will drift again.

### 19 · Energy cell: escalating price

**What.** Replace the hard `max 1/day` with an escalating same-day price —
second cell at 3×, third at 9×.

**Why.** Your read of the cell is right and the numbers back it: `+5⚡` is
**exactly `COST_BOSS_COMMIT = 5`**, so one cell is precisely one boss attempt.
It exists to turn savings into tempo when you are blocked. But a hard 1/day means
if you need six energy for two attempts, wealth cannot help — which is the exact
moment it should. Escalation holds the ceiling **economically** (nobody farms at
◈600 per 5⚡) while letting you buy the one extra attempt before you upgrade.

**Bonus.** It converts a purchase with an obvious right answer into a real
decision each time. Above floor 5 the cell is currently strictly correct to buy
every day.

**Risk.** It leans on "the wait *is* the game", so the escalation must be steep
enough to stay an emergency valve rather than a bypass.

## F · The shared world

### 20 · Does killing the frontier warden open the floor for everyone? — **needs your call**

**The finding.** `p["unlocked_floor"]` starts at 1 and is raised in exactly one
place in the codebase: **your own** warden victory (`combat.py:723`). No path
lets the world frontier raise anyone's unlocked floor. But three places say
otherwise — the Stone prints "The lift opens for everyone when a Warden falls",
worldd announces "floor N+1 is open for everyone", and `floor_level_req`'s
docstring says it exists "so a fresh climber can't ride the world lift to floor
40", describing a lift that was never wired up.

**So the SAO model appears to have been the intent, and its guardrail already
exists** — the level leash, `level ≥ floor − 10`.

**Option A — shared lift (SAO).** Killing the frontier warden opens that floor
for everyone, subject to the level leash. New players ride up but must still
level on lower floors to survive. Coordination gains a real prize, and the
announcements become true.

**Option B — personal only.** Every climber beats every warden. Then three
strings in the game are lying and need rewriting, and the shared warden is
just a co-op gold source.

**Why it matters now.** It decides what 21 and 22 are *for*. A coordination
mechanic guarding a real world-wide unlock is a raid; one guarding a gold
piñata is a chore.

### 21 · Warden wounds close after silence

**What.** The frontier warden's accumulated damage persists only while it keeps
being struck. If nobody lands a blow for N hours, the pool closes fully.

**Why this shape.** You are right that regen at hours-scale does nothing inside a
fight — what it actually tests is whether several people struck in the same
window. It is a coordination check wearing a healing costume. The problem is
**legibility**: nobody can look at `0.08 × hours_since_last_strike` and know
whether to rally. A silence timer is announceable — *"the Warden bleeds, 3
strikers in the last hour, the wound closes in 4"* — which is a Crier line and a
reason to message your guild.

**Why not a dawn reset** (my earlier suggestion): a global clock hands the
advantage to whoever lives near the reset hour. A silence window opens whenever
the first striker hits, so any group in any time zone picks its own moment.

**Depends on.** 20, for its meaning. 22, for safety.

**Reuses.** Milestone bosses already count pledges in a rolling window, so this
makes the two boss types consistent instead of inventing a third pattern.

### 22 · Coordination window scales with floor

**What.** Silence window short early (~6h), long near the top (24h+).

**Why.** Solo odds are 65–85% through floor 30 and **under 10% before floor 50**.
So below 30 a tight window is a fair challenge — players have a real
alternative. Above 50 the frontier warden **is** the only path, and a window
short enough to be exciting at floor 20 would stall the tower at floor 80 for
everyone. Same mechanic, different stakes.

### 23 · Bulwark enemies vs regeneration

**What.** Decide what regen does against `bulwark`, documented as "the
outlast-you enemy" at `BULWARK_HP_MULT = 2.2`.

**Why.** Regen is the precise counter to an enemy whose whole identity is *long
fight*. Either make it a deliberate rock-paper-scissors read, or give bulwark a
regen-suppression trait. Do not leave it accidental.

**Depends on.** 14 or 17.

## G · The long tail

### 24 · Player-built structures

**What.** Watchtowers, traps, shrines — things a rich climber builds that persist
in the world.

**Why.** `vision/ideas.md` §2 and `vision/world.md` both promise these, deferred
since `plans/003-execution/phase-5`. They are the natural gold sink for a
late-game player who has everything, and they make wealth visible — which is the
same job as 12.

**Risk.** Large, and it wants the shared-world questions (20) settled first.

### 25 · Seasons instead of a floor-100 cliff

**What.** Something that continues past Vharuk.

**Why.** WoW's endgame does not extend one ladder forever — every season
**intentionally resets** it with a new tier. Right now floors 1–100 end and
prestige is explicitly out of scope (`vision/economy.md` §5, plan 006), so floor
100 is a cliff. This is the question 10/11 eventually forces: once gear is the
ladder, a season is just a new band of it.

**Risk.** Largest thing on the list. Do not start here — but decide 10/11 with it
in mind, because a capped level plus a gear ladder is exactly the structure that
makes seasons possible later. **Superseded by 26**, which is the concrete
version of this idea.

## H · One Tower

### 26 · One Tower — shared wardens, an ending, reincarnation

**What.** The MMO redesign, in one line: **one warden per floor, all 100
shared, for the whole world** — each falls once and the floor opens for
everyone (level leash `level ≥ floor − 10` already exists). Floors 1–30 are
tuned so a single amazing player can solo the shared warden (regen below one
player's output — hard, achievable); from 31 the wound out-heals any single
blade and required strikers grow exponentially to `max(50, 10% of active
players)` at floor 100. When Vharuk falls, the era ends in shared victory,
the world resets, and every player of that era gets **one reincarnation
point** — permanent recognition on a Stone of Eras that survives resets.

**Why it earned its place.** Two findings forced it. The game currently runs
**both** warden models at once, unconnected — everyone privately re-fights all
100 wardens while the shared warden pays gold and opens nothing. And the
shared warden's coordination requirement is **flat at ~7 players from floor 1
to floor 100** — nothing scales with floor or population, so the finale is
exactly as hard as floor 10.

**How it works, mechanically.** Regen sets the minimum head count (fewer than
N/2 strikers visibly lose ground); HP sets the rally size (N full energy bars
in one window); the silence window (21) sets how simultaneous the rally must
be, widening with the floor (22). Reincarnation perks follow the law:
**prestige buys time, never power.**

**Depends on.** 13 first (the floor/level split), then it **merges with 10 and
11 into one retune** — level cap at 30, gear as the power ladder, and the
warden coordination curve are the same spreadsheet pass over the same
constants. Tune the system once, for everything.

**Absorbs.** 20 (shared lift: answered "yes, above 30"), 21 and 22 (its
coordination mechanics), 25 (seasons become eras).

**Risk.** The biggest item on this list, and the small-world edge needs a
decision: `max(50, 10%)` is impossible below ~60 active players. Full design,
worked numbers and six open decisions: `one-tower-for-everyone.md`.

### 27 · Hunting parties

**What.** Two or three players in the *same* fight — your blows and your
friend's in one round log, the monster turning on whoever drew its attention.
Parties can hunt the wilds, take contracts together, and above all fight
sieges as actual groups rather than as parallel strikers.

**Why it is the "one more thing".** One Tower makes the world shared, but the
core verb — fighting — stays solo; striking a shared HP pool is parallel play,
not together play. Every lasting MMO memory is "the night WE killed it".
Parties convert every other system on this page from adjacent play into shared
play, which makes it the highest player value on the list.

**Why it is not in the five picks.** Synchronous multi-player combat inside an
asynchronous, player-paced, chat-driven engine is the hardest thing here by a
wide margin — turn order across players, waiting rules, disconnects, reward
splits, and the energy model all need answers. It is the answer to "complexity
aside", flagged so we walk in with open eyes.

**Depends on.** 26 (it is the war's natural fighting unit). A humble v1 could
be *assist strikes*: your strike within N minutes of a friend's gains a bonus
and shares a combat log line — togetherness without synchronous rounds.
