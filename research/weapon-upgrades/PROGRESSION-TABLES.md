# Weapon progression tables

These are candidate design values for the [research proposal](/Users/roy/Documents/my-projects-docs/luna-linear-ascent/research/weapon-upgrades/RESEARCH.md). They are not deployed recipes. Floors, character levels, and weapon upgrade levels are separate. `+0` is acquisition; rows `+1` through `+20` are individual upgrade charges. Cumulative columns include acquisition within that grade only.

The attack column is the neutral weapon bonus, before the character contribution, training, type counters, wear, and optional temper. It follows the current reference curve; it does not equalize actual builds or routes.

The material column is recipe quantity **Q for each named grade**. Blade costs A=Q / B=3Q; bow costs A=3Q / B=Q; staff costs A=2Q / B=2Q. Multiply cumulative Q by the same coefficients to obtain cumulative units of each material. All quantities and gold values below are exact integers.

## Costs compared with the current reference route

The comparison sums `charge / daily_income(at that charge's gate)` and calls the result **design-income days**. It is a relative effort index, not elapsed season length. It assumes immediate eligibility, the same reference route and income on both sides, no pawn refunds, no interest, no deaths, and no materials. The candidate is deliberately close at the band level; optimized routes are allowed to be faster.

Nominal gold totals alone are misleading because payments move between floors with different incomes. This study calibrates the effort index; it does not claim identical lifetime currency sinks. The first paid Common weapon remains 200 gold. Subsequent weapon upgrades replace repeated purchases and weapon honing, including its removed XP charge.

## Material discovery and money on every floor

Percentages below apply to an ordinary common-specimen mixed carrier with no bonus. Use linear interpolation between the knots listed after the table. A/R/E/L bundle units are `Y` for each grade. Each successful grade roll gives A:B = 2Y:2Y for a mixed carrier, 3Y:Y for an A carrier, or Y:3Y for a B carrier. Carrier chance modifiers, specimens, deep hunts, and route weights create deliberately unequal outcomes. Columns are independent probabilities, not a normalized loot distribution.

Gold/kill is the economy function's current base ordinary kill quote, before actual encounter rewards, jitter, specimen, deep hunting, and fade; DI is the current design net-income estimate. It is not a flat award for every creature. The early gold law may be dominated by the gold/XP minimum rather than the pure income exponential.

## Reproduction

Source functions: `pillar`, `daily_income`, `gold_per_kill`, `_reference_bonus`, `reference_hone`, `honed_bonus`, and catalog prices from the economy module cited in the report. Generate state gates from each 25-floor grade interval, removing Common {12,13,22,23}, Rare {32,33,42,43}, Epic {52,53,62,63}, Legendary {82,83,92,93}. Index the remaining 21 floors from +0 to +20. Apply the gold/material formulas in the report. Use decimal 1.3 and 1.18 for proposed integer costs, ceiling for charges, and nearest integer with halves upward for proposed maximum durability. Existing reference stats retain the engine's own rounding.

For pacing, `K=ceil(Q/Y)`, expected kills `K/p`, and P90 is the smallest integer n such that the binomial probability of at least K successful bundles in n eligible kills is 90% or more. For the unfavorable blade/bow carrier use `K=ceil(3Q/Y)`. A future simulation of weighted trails must track A and B independently after each actual encounter.
